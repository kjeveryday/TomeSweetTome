# Successful Reading Program Blueprint

## Purpose

This document is an implementation brief for a new library reading program starting without an established audience, partner network, or operating history.

The program should be easy to explain, equitable across ages and reading abilities, usable without technology, inexpensive to operate, and measurable from its first season.

## Program Definition

### Public promise

> Read anything you enjoy on 20 days this summer.

### Recommended duration

- Eight weeks
- Participants may join at any time before the final week
- Missing days does not reset progress
- Completion requires reading on 20 distinct days, not 20 consecutive days

### Eligible reading

All of the following count:

- Print books
- Ebooks
- Audiobooks
- Comics, graphic novels, and manga
- Magazines and other sustained reading
- Fan fiction
- Books and other reading in any language
- Reading aloud to someone
- Being read to
- Repeated books, especially for early readers

Do not measure pages, titles, difficulty, or reading speed. Tracking days is more equitable across book lengths, formats, ages, abilities, and reading levels.

## Target Audiences

Support four tracks while keeping the central challenge identical:

1. Early childhood: caregivers record shared-reading days.
2. Children: participants record their own reading or shared reading.
3. Teens: all formats and self-selected material count.
4. Adults: all formats and self-selected material count.

Age tracks may have different artwork, recommendations, and events. They should not have different definitions of success.

## Product Principles

Every program decision should support these principles:

1. **Reader choice:** Participants decide what and how to read.
2. **Low friction:** Joining takes less than one minute.
3. **Universal access:** The program works without an app, library card, purchase, or regular library visit.
4. **Progress over competition:** Celebrate individual and community progress; do not publicly rank readers.
5. **Books over trinkets:** Use books, recognition, and experiences as rewards.
6. **Outreach over passive promotion:** Register people where they already gather.
7. **Consistency:** Use a predictable weekly rhythm and repeat the program annually.
8. **Measurable outcomes:** Track participation, retention, library connection, and reader experience.

## Functional Requirements

### Registration

Registration must:

- Take less than one minute
- Work on paper and digitally
- Be available inside and outside the library
- Permit late registration
- Avoid requiring a library card
- Ask only for information needed to operate and evaluate the program

Recommended fields:

- Participant name or nickname
- Age track
- Preferred contact method, optional unless operationally necessary
- Parent or caregiver consent where required
- Home branch or registration location
- Permission for public recognition, optional and off by default

Do not require demographic information during the initial sign-up. If evaluation requires it, make it optional and explain why it is collected.

### Progress tracking

Provide equivalent paper and digital trackers.

The tracker must:

- Display 20 clearly markable reading days
- Explain what kinds of reading count
- Allow staff to record progress for participants
- Tolerate lost paper logs through self-reported reconstruction
- Work with assistive technology in its digital form
- Avoid streaks, penalties, and reset mechanics

Recommended statuses:

- `registered`: registration completed
- `active`: at least one reading day or check-in reported
- `halfway`: at least 10 reading days reported
- `completed`: at least 20 reading days reported

### Participant check-in

Participants must be able to report progress through at least three routes:

- At the library
- Through the digital tracker or web form
- At an outreach event

Phone reporting is recommended when staffing permits.

### Recognition and rewards

Use three milestones:

| Milestone | Requirement | Recommended recognition |
|---|---:|---|
| Welcome | Registration | Tracker, bookmark, and welcome message |
| Halfway | 10 reading days | Badge, sticker, public-display contribution, or raffle entry |
| Completion | 20 reading days | Participant-selected book and celebration invitation |

Requirements:

- The completion reward should ideally be a book the participant chooses.
- Alternatives must be available for participants who cannot use the standard reward.
- Rewards must not depend on reading the most books or pages.
- No participant should be publicly identified without permission.
- A grand prize, if used, should be reading-related or experiential.

Avoid awarding unrelated plastic prizes for every book. Rewards should acknowledge progress without making reading feel like a transaction.

### Community goal

The program should include a shared, noncompetitive goal, such as:

> Together, our community will record 10,000 reading days.

Display progress visually in the library and digitally. Participants may add a leaf, star, book spine, creature, or other theme element for each milestone. The shared goal must not replace individual completion.

### Recommendations and discovery

The program must help participants find enjoyable material without imposing a required reading list.

Provide:

- Staff recommendations refreshed weekly
- Recommendations for each age track
- Multiple formats and languages
- High-interest, low-reading-level options
- Graphic novels, manga, audiobooks, and accessible formats
- A participant recommendation wall or equivalent social feature

### Programming cadence

Begin with a sustainable minimum schedule:

- One recurring family or all-ages program each week
- One outreach appearance each week
- One passive, self-directed activity available throughout the week

Every event should connect naturally to books or library resources. For example, a science activity should have an associated book display and a nature walk should feature field guides.

Do not launch with a large calendar that staff cannot sustain. Add targeted programming only after participation patterns and community interests become visible.

## Outreach Requirements

Because a new library has no established audience, outreach is a core operating function rather than a marketing supplement.

### Initial partner categories

Contact organizations in these categories:

- Public and private schools
- Homeschool groups
- Daycares and summer camps
- Parks, pools, and recreation programs
- Food-distribution sites
- Community centers
- Apartment communities
- Shelters
- Faith and cultural organizations
- Disability and family-support organizations

### Partner request

Keep the initial request small and concrete:

> May we have ten minutes to register families for a free summer reading program? We will bring everything and require no work from your staff.

### Outreach kit

Staff should bring:

- Paper registrations
- Paper trackers
- Pens or pencils
- A small, diverse selection of books
- Library-card applications, kept separate from program registration
- A one-page program explanation
- A QR code linking directly to digital registration
- Materials in the principal community languages

Staff should register participants on the spot. Leaving flyers alone does not count as completed outreach.

## Accessibility and Inclusion Requirements

The program must explicitly state that all formats, languages, shared reading, and repeated reading count.

Provide:

- A complete non-digital path
- Screen-reader-compatible digital materials
- Large-print materials
- Plain-language instructions
- Translations for the community's principal languages
- Audiobook and accessible-format recommendations
- Participation options for people unable to visit the library
- Events at varied times
- Late enrollment without penalty
- Reward alternatives when needed

Do not:

- Require purchases
- Require a smartphone
- Require weekly attendance
- Require public disclosure of reading ability
- Rank participants by quantity read
- Disqualify participants for losing a tracker
- Treat audiobooks or graphic novels as lesser forms of reading

## Participant Experience

### Ideal journey

1. A participant encounters the program at the library or in the community.
2. They understand the challenge in ten seconds.
3. They register in less than one minute.
4. They receive a tracker and an immediate reading recommendation.
5. They read material of their choice and record reading days.
6. They see their progress contribute to a visible community effort.
7. At 10 days, they receive lightweight recognition.
8. At 20 days, they choose a book and are invited to the celebration.
9. Before the program ends, they receive an invitation to a specific next library activity.

### Inactivity reminder

If contact permission exists, send one friendly reminder to registered participants who have not become active. The message should emphasize that they can begin or resume at any time. Do not use guilt, urgency, or streak-loss language.

## Measurement Plan

### Core counts

Track:

- Total registrations
- Active participants
- Halfway participants
- Completed participants
- Completion rate among active participants
- New library cards associated with the program
- Attendance at program events
- Participants registered through outreach
- Circulation during the program period
- Completion books distributed

Definitions must remain consistent across the season and from year to year.

### Outcome questions

Ask participants or caregivers these three questions at completion:

1. Did you read more often because of the program?
2. Did you discover something you enjoyed reading?
3. Do you feel more comfortable using the library?

Use a simple response scale such as `yes`, `somewhat`, and `no`, plus one optional open-ended comment.

### Recommended calculated metrics

- **Activation rate:** active participants / registrations
- **Completion rate:** completed participants / active participants
- **Outreach acquisition rate:** outreach registrations / registrations
- **Library conversion rate:** new library cards / participants who lacked a card at registration
- **Return rate:** participants who attend or borrow again within 60 days after the program

Do not use registration totals alone as the definition of success.

## Launch Plan

### Six to eight weeks before launch

- Confirm program dates and the 20-day goal.
- Assign one accountable program owner.
- Finalize the visual identity and plain-language rules.
- Select paper and digital tracking methods.
- Set the book and operating budgets.
- Order completion books before decorations or novelty prizes.
- Create the partner contact list.
- Establish baseline circulation and library-card data.

### Four weeks before launch

- Begin school and community visits.
- Train all public-facing staff on the ten-second explanation.
- Publish registration materials.
- Test paper and digital registration with representative users.
- Prepare accessible and translated materials.
- Begin registering participants on site at partner locations.

### Launch week

- Hold a visible but operationally manageable launch event.
- Allow immediate registration, book discovery, and first-day tracking.
- Install the community progress display.
- Confirm that all reporting routes work.

### During the eight-week program

- Maintain the recurring weekly cadence.
- Conduct at least one outreach appearance per week.
- Refresh recommendations weekly.
- Update community progress regularly.
- Send one friendly reminder to inactive registrants.
- Monitor supply, staffing, accessibility, and participation data.
- Record issues and participant questions for the next season.

### Final week

- Continue accepting new participants when meaningful participation remains possible.
- Confirm completion and distribute participant-selected books.
- Collect the three outcome questions.
- Hold a celebration that recognizes all participants rather than top-volume readers.
- Invite participants to a specific upcoming library activity.

### Within 30 days after completion

- Calculate the core metrics.
- Review feedback and accessibility issues.
- Contact partners with results and thanks.
- Document changes for the next season.
- Schedule the next edition before momentum is lost.

### Within 60 days after completion

- Measure return visits, circulation, or event attendance where privacy rules allow.
- Produce a short public impact report.

## Budget Priorities

Allocate limited funds in this order:

1. Books participants can keep
2. Staff time for outreach
3. Accessible printing and translation
4. Clear signage and visual identity
5. One memorable closing celebration
6. Optional software, performers, decorations, and novelty items

The program should remain functional if optional software or entertainment is removed.

## Staffing and Operations

Assign:

- One program owner accountable for schedule, budget, and results
- One data owner responsible for definitions, privacy, and reporting
- Named outreach contacts for each partner
- A staff member responsible for accessible materials and accommodations
- A backup for every public-facing program function

Provide every staff member with:

- The ten-second explanation
- Eligibility and completion rules
- Instructions for paper and digital registration
- Instructions for reconstructing a lost tracker
- Reward fulfillment instructions
- Accessibility and escalation guidance
- Frequently asked questions

## Minimum Viable Program

If time or money is severely constrained, the program must still include:

- The 20-day, read-anything challenge
- Paper registration and tracking
- At least one way to register outside the library
- Participant-selected completion books
- A community progress display
- One recurring weekly program
- One outreach appearance per week
- The three outcome questions

Everything else is an enhancement.

## Acceptance Criteria

The program is ready to launch only when all of the following are true:

- A new participant can explain the challenge after hearing it once.
- Registration can be completed in less than one minute.
- A person without a library card or smartphone can fully participate.
- Staff can explain exactly what counts as reading.
- Paper and digital routes produce compatible participant records.
- Lost trackers can be reconstructed without penalty.
- Completion rewards are stocked or a fulfillment plan is confirmed.
- Participants can join through at least one community location.
- Materials are accessible and available in the principal community languages.
- Staff know how to record registration, activity, halfway, and completion.
- The program owner can calculate activation and completion rates.
- Every participant receives a specific invitation to return after the program.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid these common failure modes:

- Complicated rules that require repeated explanation
- Required titles, quizzes, or book reports
- Measuring only registrations
- Giving the largest rewards to already-prolific readers
- Requiring weekly library visits
- Treating flyers as outreach
- Building the program around an app
- Spending heavily on decorations before books and access
- Scheduling more events than staff can sustain
- Ending the participant relationship at the closing celebration
- Changing goals or definitions during the program

## One-Sentence Specification

Build an all-ages, eight-week program in which anyone can register in under one minute, read anything on 20 self-selected days, track progress on paper or digitally, contribute to a shared community goal, choose a free book upon completion, and receive a clear invitation into an ongoing relationship with the library.
