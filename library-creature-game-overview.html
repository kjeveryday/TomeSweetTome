793.932 — Premise

The Premise

Kids scan the barcode of any library book and a creature comes out of it. The same book makes the same creature anywhere on Earth. What kind of creature depends on what kind of book: horror books hide little mummies, mysteries hide hounds, science books hide bookworms.

The creatures don't want to be caught. They want a place to live. A kid's growing collection of scanned books becomes a habitat, and the more a kid reads, tends, and returns books on time, the more the world fills up. The library becomes a wilderness you can walk into for free.

This sits in the lineage of games where the real world is the cartridge: Skannerz turned grocery barcodes into monsters, Monster Rancher hashed CDs into companions, Pokémon Go made street corners into spawn points. All three worked because the input was abundant, free, and deterministic, so playground knowledge-trading could flourish. It rejects the opposite lineage, Tonies and amiibo, where the "collectible" is a $15 SKU and play is pay.

WHY LIBRARY BOOKS: every book already wears two barcodes. The collectible is free by civic design. Libraries are funded to want exactly what this game produces: more youth visits, more circulation, more reading.
100.4 — Foundations

Four Core Pillars

Everything else in this document is negotiable. These four are not. Any mechanic that doesn't serve at least one pillar gets cut.

Library Books

The input, the venue, and the partner. Free, ubiquitous, kid-safe, and infinitely varied. No purchases, ever.

Creature Collecting

Deterministic generation from ISBNs. Same book, same creature, everywhere. Discovery, rarity, and completion drive the hunt.

Creature Caring

What you catch, you tend. Cozy, not punishing: bonding, feeding, habitats, and things that happen while you're away.

Reading & Learning

The invisible engine. Reading feeds growth without ever feeling like homework. The game succeeds when kids read more.

025.431 — The Input

What a Book Tells Us

The ISBN identifies the title; the library's own barcode identifies the physical copy. The ISBN is structured enough to give creature taxonomy for free, and it unlocks free metadata (Open Library, Google Books) for typing. The library barcode is an opaque per-copy ID: useless to decode, perfect for "this exact famous copy" shiny mechanics.

THE CLOSEST ANCESTOR: Monster Rancher is the only one that combined a real-world input with deep raising — and its two gaps define this game. The input lived on a console instead of in a pocket, and its care model was punishing (aging, permadeath, grief as a feature). 174.9 — Doctrine

Design Principles

  1. Capture never fails.The hash is local and deterministic. No network, no account, no purchase, no failure state at the moment of magic.
  2. Attraction over capture.The core verb is invite and tend, not hunt and defeat. Creatures choose you because of what your shelf has become.
  3. Absence is never punished.Coming back is always a pleasant surprise. Wandering off is recoverable; nothing dies because a kid had a busy week.
  4. Identity is earned, not asked.The trainer's class emerges from behavior. Demographics are replaced by an in-fiction quiz kids enjoy answering.
  5. Reading is the engine, never the chore.Anything that smells like Accelerated Reader is out. Content checks, if any, are in-fiction bonding, never gates.
  6. Design so cheating doesn't matter.In a cozy solo collector, most cheating has no victim. Protect only the communal & competitive rewards; let the rest be loose.
  7. The strictest privacy is a feature.Local-first, no PII, anonymous aggregates only. "We can't see your child's data" is marketing, not a constraint (the Seek by iNaturalist standard).
  8. The library is the hero.Every mechanic should be something a librarian would proudly put a poster up for.
021.2 — Partnerships

Library Integration: Three Trust Tiers

The partnership upgrades the game; it is never load-bearing. Each tier is a complete, shippable experience, and each verified tier unlocks rewards the tier below can't fake.

Tier 1 · Anywhere
Requires: nothing

Scan → hash → creature, enriched by free metadata. Works in any library, bookstore, or bedroom on Earth. This is the prototype and the forever-free core.

Tier 2 · Branch-Aware
Requires: goodwill + a laminated sheet

Branch totems (rotating QR at the desk) prove a real visit with zero GPS. Public catalog data flavors local spawns. Receipt scans softly verify checkouts. Branch galleries open.

Tier 3 · Partnered
Requires: formal partnership, library holds patron data

Checkout-aware mechanics via the library's ILS: hatch at checkout, evolve on on-time return, holds as rare summons. The library, not the game, owns the patron relationship.

CHICAGO REFERENCE STACK: CPL runs BiblioCommons (discovery) over a Polaris ILS with an available API — a stack shared by many major US urban systems, so nothing here is a one-city build. The CPL Foundation has a track record of funding patron-experience tech. A working Tier 1 that visibly pulls kids into the stacks IS the pitch.
025.6 — Circulation Systems

Technical Rollout: The Partnership Path

Library lending is two separate ecosystems, and this game is lucky enough to live entirely in the open one. Digital lending (Libby, Hoopla) is vendor-gated content licensing where private-equity-owned platforms control the pipes. Physical lending is library-owned inventory managed by rented software, held together by decades-old open protocols with no licensing chokepoint.

Because the game reads barcodes off physical books and never lends content, it can skip the entire digital stack: no OverDrive partner agreement, no publisher negotiations, no existing at a platform's pleasure. The integration surface we need — patron auth and circulation events — is exactly the part of library tech that is open, standardized, and library-controlled.

Realities to respect

The library is the customer everywhere in this industry; patrons pay nothing, so every model is B2B or B2B2C, sales cycles run at municipal-procurement speed, and budgets are flat. The pitch that fits that reality is the one this game was designed around: a circulation-and-visits driver the library brands as its own, with pilot metrics (youth checkouts, branch visits via totem scans) that map directly onto what libraries already report to their funders.

Patron authentication is the industry's graveyard: there is no universal library identity layer, and startups have died trying to become one. The trust-tier architecture is the shield here — the game never holds library credentials, never depends on auth to function, and lets each partner library own the patron relationship entirely. Target selection is also a solved research problem: ILS and digital-vendor deployments per library are publicly trackable (Marshall Breeding's Library Technology Guides is the canonical map), so the Phase 3 and 4 pipeline can be built from a desk.

FILED FOR LATER: if the game ever touches ebooks, the open path exists — the Palace Project's open-source stack and the OPDS/ODL/LCP standards are the library-governed side of digital lending. Physical-first remains the strategy; this is a bookmark, not a plan.
364.16 — Integrity

Honesty by Design

The reframe: don't ask "how do we stop cheating," ask "which rewards must be earned honestly." Shared ISBN lists were the social fabric of Skannerz and Monster Rancher — a kid speedrunning their own museum has no victim. Verification spends effort only where a victim exists.

001.4 — Unresolved

Open Questions

Held deliberately open. These get answered by prototyping and by the first library conversation, not by this document.

  • Platform: kid's own device, family shared device, or (Skannerz-style) something dedicated? Shapes session design, COPPA posture, and who "owns" the collection.
  • Contest vs. combat: does battling exist at all, or do Ooblets-style playful contests carry the competitive itch? Skannerz DNA argues for stakes; the library venue argues for tone.
  • Business model: the anti-Tonies stance rules out pay-to-play. What sustains it — grants, library licensing, philanthropy, cosmetics for grown-ups?
  • Solo vs. social at v1: branch galleries and trading are powerful but multiply moderation and verification cost. What's the smallest lovable social layer?
  • Role of reading verification: how far past "sustained possession" do we go before it starts smelling like homework?
  • The name. Pending, and enjoying being pending.
